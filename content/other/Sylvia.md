My work is relational, depth-oriented, and grounded in the belief that meaningful change happens when people feel safe enough to understand themselves differently.

Over time, people often find themselves reacting less automatically, relating more honestly, and feeling more connected to themselves and the lives they've built. Not because they became someone new — but because the relationship they have with themselves has changed.

There is no single path through this work. What I use depends on you — where you are, what you're carrying, and what your system needs to shift.

How the work unfolds

Becoming a therapist was not my original plan — but understanding people always was.

I've long been drawn to what lives beneath the surface — the patterns we repeat, the emotions we carry, the ways life shapes us before we fully realize it.

I came to this work as a human first, and a therapist second. Parts of my own journey felt difficult to make sense of at times. What made the difference was the support of others — a small but meaningful village I remain deeply grateful for. It taught me something I now see every day in this work: people heal through connection, not isolation.

That understanding shapes how I work. The emotions we avoid are often connected to the patterns that keep us stuck. Not something to push through, but something worth getting curious about.

After years of sitting with people in some of their hardest moments, I've learned that change is possible, even when it doesn't feel that way at first. Not all at once, and not through willpower or performance — but gradually, in ways that begin to feel real and lasting.

Nature does not rush, yet everything is accomplished.

Outside the therapy room

Outside of the therapy room, I go looking for mossy patches. I slow down in the ways I always have — in nature, often with my family beside me. We travel when we can, usually drawn to deep green forests, old trees, and a good local meal we'll later try to recreate at home. I've also taken up pencil drawing, with results that continue to keep me humble.

I've come to value the quiet in between — a morning stretch, waves nearby, the first signs of something new growing. Those moments matter. Because presence in the therapy room starts with being present in my own life.

What your first session looks like

There’s no clipboard, no intake form marathon, no pressure to “tell me everything.” We start where you are.

I’ll ask a few questions. You’ll talk. I’ll listen — not just to the words, but to what’s underneath them. By the end of our first hour, you’ll have a clearer sense of whether this space feels right.

Most people say they feel lighter after the first session. Not because anything was solved, but because they finally felt heard without having to perform.
