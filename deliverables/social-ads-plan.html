Every Instagram account, the TikTok account and every Google campaign gets the same $1,000 for the month, about $33 a day. Each Instagram account splits its $1,000 evenly between messages and its landing page. If month one works, month two repeats the same $13,000, with more of it behind whatever did best.

Who sees the ads

Three kinds of people

New travelers like our past guests

Instagram finds people who look like the guests we already have, using our guest list as the starting point.

New travelers who fit the trip

Adults in the US and Canada interested in luxury travel, steered by story: families for Gardens and Tented, couples for Springs, and travelers drawn to islands, culture or the night sky for Bocas, Hangaroa and Atacama.

People who already showed interest

Anyone who watched most of a video, followed along on Instagram, or visited our site in the last month sees a reminder.

Guests who stayed with us in the past year are left out, so we do not pay to reach people who already know us.

How we will know it works

We count bookings, not likes

Every link is labeled

Each ad and each link our team sends carries a label, so a booking can be traced back to the video that started it.

Every conversation is logged

Our team keeps a simple record of each message: which property, whether it was a real inquiry, and whether it became a booking.

We judge on real results

The main question is simple: for every dollar spent, how much did guests book? We judge the channels together, property by property, because a guest often sees a video first and searches on Google later.

Two months, one decision point

Month one proves it, month two builds on it

  1. Before launchConnect the accounts, check that every landing page records visits and bookings, make sure Google counts bookings correctly on every account, and set up the welcome message our team uses.
  2. Weeks 1 and 2Let the ads run untouched. Instagram, TikTok and Google need this time to learn who responds.
  3. Week 3Pause the videos and searches that are not pulling their weight so the rest of each $1,000 goes behind the best ones.
  4. End of month oneThe decision point. Month one counts as a success if the bookings it brought in are worth more than the $13,000 spent, or if enough real inquiries are in progress to clearly get there. We also see which accounts did best, and whether messages or landing pages worked better.
  5. Month twoIf month one works, another $13,000 goes in, shifted toward the strongest accounts, routes and videos. If it does not, we stop and keep what we learned. Trips are often booked weeks ahead, so bookings keep being counted after the ads stop.
