Executive summary

Why I am proposing this

I do not believe our websites, as they are built today, are designed to turn a visitor into a booking. They show the properties well, but someone who searches for a family trip with sloths, or for overwater villas, lands on a general homepage and has to find their own way. Many never do.

This plan works around that. Nobody who taps one of these ads is sent to our main websites. Every ad leads to one of two places built for that person:

  • A landing page about exactly what they were looking for, with a direct link into our booking engine.
  • A conversation with a real person on our team, who can answer questions and send a booking link.

We start on November 2 with $12,000 a month across five properties, using the videos that have already performed best on our own accounts. On Google, searches for our own name are blocked, so we only pay to reach new travelers. After one month we decide, based on bookings, whether to run a second month.

Who sees the ads

Three kinds of people

New travelers like our past guests

Instagram finds people who look like the guests we already have, using our guest list as the starting point.

New travelers who fit the trip

Adults in the US and Canada interested in luxury travel, steered by story: families for Tented, couples for Springs, and travelers drawn to islands, culture or the night sky for Bocas, Hangaroa and Atacama.

People who already showed interest

Anyone who watched most of a video, followed along on Instagram, or visited our site in the last month sees a reminder.

Guests who stayed with us in the past year are left out, so we do not pay to reach people who already know us.

How organic helps

Proven videos, working twice

We are not making new content for this. Each account runs one reel: the one that has already performed best on our own Instagram and TikTok, so we start with what we know people like. Keeping it to one reel per account also keeps the plan simple to run and easy to read. Our own posts, email and Google listings then support the ads for free.

Ads run from the original posts

Each ad is built from the original post, so it keeps the views, likes and comments it has already earned. Travelers trust a video that others have clearly enjoyed.

Reshared with an invitation to talk

We reshare the same reels in Stories and as fresh posts with a new caption and a simple prompt, like "Reply SLOTH for details." Every reply becomes a conversation with our team.

The best video pinned to each profile

When someone taps through from an ad to our profile, the first thing they see is the video that matches what caught their eye, with the landing page linked in the bio.

The same stories to our own guests

Past family guests get the family stories by email, past couples get the romance story. People who already know us are the quickest to book again.

Google listings that point to the pages

Each property posts its story on its Google Business Profile, linking to its landing page, so travelers planning on Google Maps find it too.

Kept separate in the results

Organic posts and emails get their own link labels, so bookings from them are counted apart from the ads and the month-two decision stays clear.

Google search, in more detail

The searches each campaign will answer

Each Google campaign is built around a few groups of related searches. When someone types one of these, our ad for that property can appear, and a tap takes them to its landing page. These are our starting list. Before launch we check each one in Google's keyword tool to confirm people actually search it, and drop or add terms based on what we find.

The most important rule: searches for our name never reach these pages

Our current Tented, Springs and Gardens campaigns are meant to reach new travelers, but 90% or more of the people who arrive through them actually searched for Nayara by name. Those people already knew us. That makes the campaigns look like they find new guests when they mostly do not.

So every new Google campaign blocks searches for our names. If someone searches "Nayara," they will not see these ads or land on these pages. Only people searching for a kind of trip, like sloths, honeymoons or overwater villas, get here. That is the only way to see what reaching new travelers is really worth.

We never pay for our own name

Every campaign blocks searches that include Nayara, our property names and our former names. Those people already know us, and our existing brand campaigns look after them. Without this block, most of the budget would go to people who were already looking for us.

One campaign per property

Each property has its own campaign and its own searches, so our campaigns never bid against each other and push up our own costs.

The list keeps getting sharper

Every week we look at the exact searches people typed before tapping our ads, block the ones that are not a fit, and add the good ones we missed.

How we will know it works

We count bookings, not likes

Every link is labeled

Each ad and each link our team sends carries a label, so a booking can be traced back to the video that started it.

Every conversation is logged

Our team keeps a simple record of each message: which property, whether it was a real inquiry, and whether it became a booking.

We judge on real results

The main question is simple: for every dollar spent, how much did guests book? We judge the channels together, property by property, because a guest often sees a video first and searches on Google later.

How Alex sets up Google

Google needs a little more detail than the social campaigns, so here it is step by step.

  1. Build new campaigns, and keep the existing ones out of the way

    Tented already has a family campaign and Springs a romance campaign. Today, 90% or more of the people arriving through those campaigns searched for Nayara by name. We recommend new campaigns rather than reworking those, with every search for our names blocked, so the results show only new travelers and every click goes to a landing page. While the test runs, the matching family and romance searches in those two campaigns are paused so our own campaigns do not bid against each other. Brand campaigns stay exactly as they are.

  2. Build the five Google search campaigns

    One campaign per property, in that property's Google Ads account, using the ad groups and searches in the Google section above, with every ad sending people to that property's landing page. Search network only, US and Canada, $1,000 a month each (about $33 a day). Start with Maximize Clicks and a cap of about $4 to $5 per click, then switch to Maximize Conversions once a campaign has around 15 tracked bookings or "Check availability" taps.

  3. Block our own names in every search campaign

    This is the most important step. One shared list of negative keywords, attached to all five campaigns: Nayara, each property name, our former names and common misspellings. After launch, a weekly look at the searches people typed, blocking any that are not a fit.

  4. Write the search ads in our own language

    One ad per group of searches, with "Check availability" as the call to action. We bid on "glamping" and "overwater bungalows," but the ads say "luxury tented camp" and "overwater villas." Real photos only for image extras, and only verified facts in the ad text. Every link, including the extra links under the ad, goes to the landing page, never to our main websites.

  5. Build the YouTube campaign

    One Demand Gen campaign on the Nayara Resorts Google Ads account, YouTube only, $1,000 a month. One ad group per property, each using both the vertical and horizontal video and linking to its own landing page. Audiences built from each property's search list, plus people similar to our past guests.

  6. Make Google count bookings correctly

    Link Google Analytics to each Google Ads account and bring in the booking event, with its value, as the main conversion. Google Ads does not record booking value correctly for Springs today, so that comes first.

What success looks like

Breaking even is a win. Doubling it is success.

On Judgment Day we look at how much guests booked through these ads, compared with the $12,000 we spent. Even if we only break even, the month has paid for itself and taught us a lot about what works. This is our proposal, and the team can adjust it before launch.

Why 2 times is the bar

The team is already in place, so the only cost is the ad money. Doubling it is a clear win, and anything above that is gravy.

Only new travelers count

We count bookings traced to these ads through our labeled links, David's message log and Google Analytics. Searches for our own name are already blocked.

Each account is judged too

The same bar applies to each property, so one slow account does not sink the others, and a strong one can keep going on its own.

Trips are often booked weeks after someone first sees an ad. On Judgment Day we count confirmed bookings plus inquiries that are clearly on their way to a booking, and we do a final count on January 2.

Two months, one decision point

Month one proves it, month two builds on it

  1. By November 2Everything in place before a single ad runs: accounts, tracking, landing pages and our messaging team ready. If any piece is not ready, we move the date rather than run ads we cannot measure.
  2. Nov 2 to 15Let the ads run untouched. Instagram, TikTok and Google need this time to learn who responds.
  3. Nov 16 to 22Check each account, route and search group. Pause the searches that are not pulling their weight, and swap in a backup reel on any account where the first one has clearly stalled.
  4. December 2: Judgment DayThe decision point. We compare what guests booked through these ads with what we spent, using the success levels above, and see which accounts did best and whether messages or landing pages worked better.
  5. DecemberIf month one reaches the success bar, another $12,000 goes in, shifted toward the strongest accounts and routes, with fresh reels where people have seen the first one too often. If it pays for itself but falls short of the bar, only the strongest accounts and routes keep going. Below that, we stop and keep what we learned.
